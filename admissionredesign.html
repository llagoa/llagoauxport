Nubo Admissions Redesign

Redesigning a high-friction admissions process into a scalable, conversion-driven onboarding experience. Reduced drop-offs from ~60% to ~12% by transforming a long static form into a guided, user-centered journey.

Preview

Context

At Nubo, the admissions process was a major growth bottleneck. Around 60% of applicants dropped off before completing the sign-up, directly limiting the number of enrolled students and impacting the organization’s ability to sustain partnerships and operational investments.

The experience relied on a 60+ question Google Form, creating high cognitive load and a discouraging first interaction. At the same time, Growth stakeholders depended heavily on this data for reporting and decision-making.

The challenge was to significantly reduce friction without compromising essential data collection.

Persona

To better understand the challenges of our users, we created Helena, a senior high school student who works part-time as a sales assistant in São Paulo. She dreams of going to college but struggles with a tight schedule and the stress of preparing for the ENEM exam.

  • Objectives: find a prep course that fits her routine, complete the admissions process, and reach her dream college.
  • Pain Points: long and exhausting sign-up forms, balancing studies with work, and the lack of guidance during stressful moments.

Helena’s profile helped us frame the admissions redesign with empathy: simplifying the flow, cutting unnecessary friction, and making the experience feel like a guided journey rather than a bureaucratic form.

Objective

  • Increase completion rate to at least 80%.
  • Reduce friction and perceived effort in the first interaction.
  • Maintain critical data collection for Growth and institutional needs.
  • Deliver a feasible solution within a 2-month timeline.

Problem Analysis & Strategy

Based on benchmarking (Duolingo, Descomplica, Busuu), we identified that successful onboarding flows minimize upfront effort and progressively collect data.

Working closely with Growth stakeholders, we audited all admission questions and classified them into: gating (required for approval) and informational (non-blocking).

This process revealed internal tension, as Growth initially resisted removing questions due to their importance for reporting and external requirements.

After multiple alignment sessions, we defined a two-step approach:

  • Step 1: 16 essential questions for approval.
  • Step 2: Additional questions collected post-approval.

This allowed us to reduce friction at the most critical moment while preserving business needs.

Design Process

The onboarding flow was structured into clear blocks: account creation, personal information, address, and socioeconomic data. This reduced cognitive load and improved clarity.

Instead of a static form, the experience was designed as a guided journey, supported by Cloudinha, helping reduce anxiety during a high-pressure moment for students.

I defined flows, wireframes, and high-fidelity interfaces, ensuring consistency through reusable components and scalable patterns.

Validation & Iteration

Due to time constraints, validation relied on internal testing and qualitative feedback rather than formal usability tools.

We conducted multiple testing sessions with developers, Growth team members, leadership, and educators to identify usability issues and friction points.

These sessions helped refine question order, clarity, and flow structure before launch.

After launch, small iterations were introduced to accommodate new business needs, such as additional questions for mid-year applicants.

Stakeholders & Collaboration

I partnered closely with the CTO and Growth team, leading UX decisions and facilitating alignment across stakeholders.

One of the main challenges was stakeholder resistance to reducing the number of questions, as Growth teams relied heavily on this data.

Through multiple discussions, I helped define what was truly necessary for approval versus what could be collected later, balancing user experience and business needs.

The project followed a weekly iterative cycle, similar to agile sprints, ensuring continuous delivery and alignment.

Interactions & Error States

Beyond the happy path, we carefully designed error states and microinteractions. These details help students understand what went wrong, how to fix it quickly, and reduce frustration during a stressful admission moment.

Cloudinha as a Guide

Inspired by Duo from Duolingo, Cloudinha was integrated as a friendly companion throughout the journey. She introduced herself to students, encouraged them to keep going, and made the process feel less lonely during a stressful moment.

Technical constraints limited the number of poses and placements we could implement, but even small appearances made a significant emotional impact.

Challenges & Trade-offs

  • Tight deadline: delivery between January and March.
  • Stakeholder resistance to reducing data collection.
  • Technical limitations (no animations or gamification).
  • Balancing user experience with institutional requirements.

Post-Approval Profile Questions

After the first step, 20 additional questions were relocated to the student’s profile. These were informative fields essential for Growth and Data, but not eliminatory. To avoid overwhelming students, we grouped them into four thematic blocks:

  • Education Questions — 5 items.
  • Questions About You — 7 items.
  • Interaction Questions — 5 items.
  • Retention Policies — 3 items.

Each block could be completed separately, with autosave and progress indicators. A visual tracker at the top showed current progress, and each finished section turned green with a checkmark. Students had until June 25 to complete all blocks, alongside the SimulaNubo and Module Nubo activities required for final enrollment.

Admissions Growth Over the Years

Admissions at Nubo have grown significantly over the past few years. The redesigned onboarding helped support this scale while maintaining a high completion rate and a much simpler experience for students.

  • 2023: 1,470 students completed the process (Google Form).
  • 2024: 1,446 students completed the process (Google Form).
  • 2025: 3,415 students completed the redesigned onboarding.
  • 2026: 7,062 students completed onboarding inside the redesigned app flow.

Results

  • Reduced drop-off from ~60% to ~12% (~88% completion rate).
  • 8,015 users started the onboarding process.
  • 7,062 users completed onboarding.
  • ~3 minutes average completion time.
  • Improved perceived usability and reduced user frustration.
  • Maintained full data collection through post-onboarding flows.
  • Enabled scalable growth without increasing operational complexity.

Learnings

  • Simplifying often means cutting — less is truly more.
  • Frequent alignment with engineering prevents rework.
  • Even under a CTO-led bridge, clear UX feedback accelerates decisions.
  • Empathy matters most when students are under pressure.
  • Redistribution of data questions post-approval can balance user needs with business intelligence.

My Role

I led the end-to-end product design process, from problem framing to final delivery.

I was responsible for defining the onboarding strategy, aligning stakeholders, and making key decisions to balance user experience with business constraints.

I designed the flows, interfaces, and overall experience, while working closely with engineering and leadership to ensure feasibility and delivery within a tight timeline.
